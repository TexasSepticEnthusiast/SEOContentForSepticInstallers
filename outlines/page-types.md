# Six useful business-site page outlines

Choose pages that reflect the actual business. These outlines preserve the original pack's core page types; they are not a requirement to create six pages or an excuse to advertise services the installer does not perform. Use the [content brief](../templates/content-brief.md) before drafting.

## Home page

Open with the verified service, the useful geographic context, and a working contact route. Then help visitors choose between service details, early planning information, and contacting the business. Explain a real differentiator only if the owner can support it. Link to fuller detail instead of making the home page answer every septic question.

Evidence to gather: business name, service scope, contact details, current coverage, and permission for any real project image. Avoid an invented founding story or unverified experience total.

## Service area page

Explain where the business currently considers work and how a customer confirms a particular address. Distinguish a working area from a boundary guarantee. Include exclusions or travel constraints only when the owner confirms them.

Local detail should help a decision: for example, how to identify the property's actual county before an inquiry. Do not create dozens of pages by replacing town names. The [permit example](../examples/guides/septic-permit-questions.md) shows one way to add researched local value without duplicating a sales page.

## Service page

Name one verified service. Explain who should inquire, what information helps, which responsibilities must be established, and what happens next. Link to a related guide for readers who are still planning. State exclusions where they prevent a wrong inquiry.

Use the [service worksheet](../templates/service-page.md) and [installation example](../examples/service/septic-installation.md). Do not add pages for pumping, aerobic work, evaluation, design, or inspections solely because the terms attract searches. Confirm the actual service and relevant qualifications first.

## About page

Introduce the real installer in an owner-approved voice. Explain their role and business approach with verifiable detail. If including credentials, verify the holder, class, status, and scope. If including past work or years of experience, document what the claim counts.

A photo, story, or quotation needs permission and accurate attribution. An installer profile must not turn into a fictional first-person persona. Link to service and contact pages so the biography supports the visitor's next decision.

## FAQ page

Answer questions the business actually receives or label the topic selection as editorial. Give a direct answer, explain any limit, and link to deeper detail where needed. Keep property-specific permit, system choice, and pricing questions tied to the person or authority who can resolve them.

Use the [FAQ worksheet](../templates/faq-page.md). Do not repeat the same keyword-heavy answer under multiple town names or invent customer quotations to introduce questions.

## Contact page

Provide verified ways to contact the business, current hours if confirmed, and a short description of useful inquiry information. Explain what the form asks for and what a customer can expect only when the business approves that process.

Collect only information needed for the inquiry. Do not ask people to publish parcel documents or personal contact details in repository issues. Test the actual form and phone/email links before website publication; this Markdown pack does not implement them.

## When a guide is useful

A guide earns its place by helping with a distinct task: gathering property information, comparing scope, or finding the correct authority. A documented job story can also be useful when evidence and permission exist. A publishing schedule or generic list of septic keywords is not enough reason to add a page.

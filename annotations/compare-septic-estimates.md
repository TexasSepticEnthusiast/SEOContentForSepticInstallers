# Teaching notes: comparing installation estimates

**Article:** [Compare septic estimates](../examples/guides/compare-septic-estimates.md)


These notes are editorial guidance for the library. Keep them separate from customer-facing article copy when adapting the sample.

- **Audience:** A Texas property owner or builder comparing bids or preparing to request them.
- **Search intent:** Informational with a later hiring decision. The page answers "how to compare septic installation estimates" instead of inventing a regional average price.
- **Suggested title element:** `How to compare septic installation estimates | Out West Septic`. The brand is included because it is the selected example, not an independent recommendation.
- **Suggested meta description:** `Compare septic installation estimates by scope, site work, permits, allowances, exclusions, and handover. Know what to ask before choosing a bid.` This is suggested page metadata, not a promise of the snippet a search engine will show.
- **Heading logic:** Establish a common project basis, compare inclusions, resolve uncertain charges, then agree on completion and the next action. The comparison table is the practical tool readers can reuse.
- **SEO rationale:** Google recommends useful original content that helps an intended audience complete a task, and descriptive titles without repetitive keyword phrases. Here, the original contribution is the scope-comparison workflow, not an unsupported claim of field experience. See Google's [people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) and [title-link guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link). This structure does not guarantee indexing, rankings, or AI citations.
- **Internal links:** The evaluation link leads to the planning example, and the authority discussion links to the permit questions guide. On an installer's own website, map these to relevant live resources rather than editorial notes.
- **CTA:** One natural brand link offers a next step after the article has answered the comparison question. It makes no claim about free quotes, response times, prices, or accepted projects.
- **How another installer adapts it:** Replace the business name and destination with their own verified information. Have the person responsible for estimating review the comparison questions. Add real scope definitions and documented exceptions from that business's process, with permission; do not fabricate a job story or publish customer documents. Update the permitting reference for the jurisdiction. A new county name alone is not a reason to duplicate this page.
- **Business-owner review needed:** Confirm the CTA suits the current intake process and decide whether to add an approved, anonymized scope example. This sample intentionally supplies no business price, payment policy, warranty, service boundary, or project-completion promise.

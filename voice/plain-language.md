# Plain language and honest claims

Keep the original pack's practical voice: write the way an installer explains a job to a neighbor. Use short sentences when the point is simple. Explain unfamiliar terms, then use them consistently.

## Say what you can support

- Name the actual service. Explain what the reader can send, ask, or decide next.
- Use real place names when they matter to the answer. A place name does not establish county-wide service or uniform soil conditions.
- Use first person only for an authorized business speaker who approves the statements. Educational examples can describe the chosen business openly without pretending to be its owner or customer.
- Add a license class and number only after verifying the current record and the relevant scope. Do not repeat a credential as a substitute for explaining the service.
- Cite the relevant official guidance. Check the actual rule before naming a section; a precise-looking but incorrect citation is worse than a useful link to the right authority.
- State uncertainty plainly: what information is missing, who can resolve it, and what decision depends on it.

## Cut these habits

Avoid em dashes, title case headings, forced groups of three, and “not just X but Y” constructions. Skip words such as “robust,” “leverage,” “delve,” “tapestry,” and “showcase” when a direct verb will do. Remove “trusted local experts,” “unmatched quality,” and similar claims unless the sentence contains meaningful evidence.

Do not invent job history to make copy sound human. Do not imply that first-person writing, a regulation number, or a local place name guarantees a search ranking or an AI citation.

## Edit toward a concrete task

| Weak draft | More useful direction |
| --- | --- |
| We offer unmatched septic solutions. | Name the verified service and explain what information a customer should provide. |
| Every rural property can use this system. | Explain that evaluated site conditions and applicable requirements determine the options. |
| We handle everything. | Identify the confirmed responsibilities and tell the reader which tasks need clarification. |
| The best septic installer in the region. | Describe an actual documented process or remove the unsupported comparison. |

These are editing directions, not substitute business claims. Read the finished copy aloud, then check every statement that could affect a customer's decision against the [source register](../docs/source-register.md) or new documented evidence.

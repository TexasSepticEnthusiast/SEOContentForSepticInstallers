# Conventional septic installation: prepare your project inquiry

> Educational service-page example using Out West Septic. This is sample copy, not a project offer, site design, or permit approval. [Read the teaching notes](../../annotations/septic-installation.md).

A new septic project starts with the property and the planned use. Before choosing a system or comparing a total price, gather enough information for an installer to understand where you are building and which questions are still open.

[Out West Septic](https://outwestseptic.com/) advertises conventional gravity septic installation for rural homes and new builds around Palo Pinto County. Ask the business to confirm that your property's location and proposed project fit its current work. A service inquiry is the start of that discussion, not a determination that a particular system will be suitable.

## Send a short project brief

You do not need to write a technical specification to start a conversation. A concise brief can separate known facts from decisions that still need professional input:

- Property location and county, with a parcel reference or survey if available.
- Whether this is a new home, a replacement system, or a change to an existing property.
- Intended use of the home and any future buildings you want to discuss with the planning team.
- Existing site evaluation, system plans, permit correspondence, or records of an older system.
- Photos of access routes and the proposed work area, taken without opening or entering any septic component.
- Your construction sequence and target dates, clearly identified as planning goals.

Label missing records as missing. It is more useful to say the house location is still under discussion than to send a sketch that looks final when it is not. If you are still comparing properties, start with [planning septic before building](../guides/planning-septic-before-building.md).

## Keep system suitability separate from the service request

TCEQ explains that an on-site sewage facility is designed around a site evaluation and local conditions. Its [septic-system basics](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/assistance/water/fyiossfs.html) are a useful starting point for understanding why a neighbor's system is not a design for your lot.

Use the first discussion to establish which documents are needed, who will prepare them, and how the proposed installation relates to the property's evaluation and approved planning material. Do not assume that someone offering installation also provides every evaluation, design, or permitting service.

If the evaluated site calls for a different type of system, ask which qualified provider should handle that next step. The goal is a project appropriate to the property, rather than forcing the property to match a preferred service.

## Ask what belongs in the written scope

An installation discussion should eventually lead to a scope you can read and compare. Ask the provider to identify the proposed work, the documents it relies on, and anything priced separately. Useful questions include:

- Does the scope include only the septic installation, or also particular preparation and restoration work?
- Which work will be performed by the provider, and which needs another contractor?
- Who is coordinating applications, inspections, and communication with the permitting authority?
- What site conditions remain uncertain, and how would a change be documented and approved?
- Which records and operating information will be handed over at completion?

These are questions to resolve, not statements about what any quoted price includes. The [estimate comparison guide](../guides/compare-septic-estimates.md) provides a way to compare answers without treating the lowest headline total as the whole project.

## Put approval questions ahead of construction dates

Confirm the applicable authority and required steps before committing to construction. The [permit questions guide](../guides/septic-permit-questions.md) helps organize that conversation. A requested start date and an installer's availability do not establish permission to begin work.

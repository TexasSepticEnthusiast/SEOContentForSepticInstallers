# Septic permit questions to ask before scheduling installation

> Educational guide using Out West Septic as the example business. This is a question-preparation resource, not an application, permit determination, or approval timetable. [Read the teaching notes](../../annotations/septic-permit-questions.md).

You have a property, a proposed project, and perhaps an installer in mind. The next useful step is to identify the authority responsible for the property and learn what it needs to review. A city name in a mailing address or a contractor's service area does not establish the answer.

Texas calls a septic system an on-site sewage facility, or OSSF. TCEQ's [permit guidance](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/ossf/ossfpermits.html) directs owners to the local permitting authority and notes that local programs can be more stringent than state minimums. Ask that authority about your actual project before treating a general online answer as permission to proceed.

## Prepare a property-specific introduction

Have the location, county, parcel information, and a plain description of the proposed work ready. Say whether you are planning a new installation, replacing a system, changing an existing system, or investigating a property with incomplete records.

Bring any survey, previous permit, evaluation, plan, or authority correspondence you have. Separate existing documents from proposed changes. If you do not know the current system's type or status, say so rather than guessing from a seller's description.

A useful opening question is: “Does your office handle this parcel and this proposed work? If another authority handles it, where should I start?” Record the answer and the date. An organized record makes later conversations easier to follow.

## Use official local starting points

These are two researched examples, not a complete directory and not interchangeable county rules. Sources checked on 2026-10-18:

| Property area | Official starting point | What to confirm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palo Pinto County | [Public Works Department](https://www.co.palo-pinto.tx.us/page/PublicWorksDepartment) | The county's published OSSF process calls for an application, planning material, site evaluation results, and the appropriate fee before authorization to construct. Confirm the current packet and applicability to your property. |
| Hood County | [Environmental Health: OSSF](https://hoodcounty.texas.gov/departments/environmental_health/onsite_sewage_facilities_%28ossf%29.php) | The page lists permitting procedures and application resources and states a permit requirement for installation, modification, and replacement. Confirm the current process for your project with that office. |

For a property elsewhere, begin with TCEQ's guidance and its linked authority lookup. Do not copy a fee, form, exemption, or instruction from the nearest familiar county. Even a correct webpage can be the wrong one for a particular parcel.

## Turn the call into a responsibilities list

Ask the authority which documents apply and how they should be submitted. Then review the resulting tasks with the people involved in the project. Questions worth resolving include:

1. Which application and planning documents are current for this type of work?
2. Who may prepare the required evaluation and planning material?
3. Who will submit the application, answer review questions, and pay applicable fees?
4. What authorization must be issued before work begins?
5. Which inspections or notifications apply, who requests them, and what work must remain visible?
6. What completion or operating documentation should the owner retain?
7. If the layout or proposed work changes, what needs another review?

The list is a conversation aid. It does not assign these responsibilities to the installer or promise that every project follows the same sequence. Keep the answers with your project records and reflect the agreed responsibilities in the written scope.

## Resolve assumptions before setting the calendar

Ask how to find current fees and whether the authority gives a review-time estimate. Treat any estimate as information to confirm for your submission, not a guaranteed completion date. If someone says acreage or an older installation removes the need for approval, ask the authority to confirm applicability before relying on that statement.

If you are comparing contractor proposals, use the [estimate comparison guide](compare-septic-estimates.md) to check whether they assume the same application and inspection responsibilities. If the layout is still fluid, the [site-planning guide](planning-septic-before-building.md) helps organize the unresolved property questions.

## Bring the answers into the installation discussion

When contacting [Out West Septic](https://outwestseptic.com/), share the property location, proposed work, and relevant authority correspondence. Ask whether the project fits the business's current scope and who would handle each remaining task. A useful installation discussion connects the provider's work to the authority's actual requirements; it does not substitute for those requirements.

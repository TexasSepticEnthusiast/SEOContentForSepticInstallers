# Write one useful septic page at a time

This workflow explains the choices behind the examples. It is an editorial method, not a ranking formula. Sources checked: 2026-10-18. See the [source register](source-register.md) for evidence and limits.

## Start with an answerable decision

Write down what the reader needs to do next. “Prepare a scope-comparison sheet before choosing an estimate” is a useful assignment. “Rank for every septic keyword in seven counties” is not an article brief.

Use actual customer questions when the business has permission to use them. Otherwise describe the topic as an editorial hypothesis. This library has not measured search volume, keyword difficulty, conversions, or customer behavior; its search-intent labels are reasoned editorial choices.

Choose the page type deliberately:

| Type | Useful outcome | Proof needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Service page | Decide whether to contact this business and what to send | Current service scope, real contact route, owner-approved process |
| Planning guide | Prepare a better question or project brief | Relevant official guidance and a practical original checklist |
| Local resource | Find the correct office or local procedure | Current jurisdiction-specific official source and review date |
| Case study | Understand a documented project and its limitations | Owner permission, real records, consent for identifying details and images |

A case study is intentionally absent here: there are no approved project records in this library.

## Build a claim ledger before writing

For each consequential claim, record the source, date checked, exact proposition it supports, and limits. Separate four kinds of material:

- Official requirements: explain narrowly and link to the responsible authority. Never turn one county's procedure into a statewide promise.
- Business facts: verify against public business material and obtain owner confirmation before publication as company copy.
- Editorial advice: identify it as a suggested way to organize a decision, rather than a regulation or the company's contractual process.
- Unverified details: keep them in the editorial brief or review checklist, outside the article body.

Write from the claim ledger in your own words. A source link is not permission to copy a source's prose. Quote sparingly if a precise short phrase matters; otherwise summarize the relevant point and add original utility.

## Draft the answer before the pitch

State the problem and the next useful action in the opening. Use headings that follow the reader's task: gather records, establish responsibilities, compare scope, ask a question. Include a practical output such as a short checklist, a comparison table, or a call-preparation note.

The [planning example](../examples/guides/planning-septic-before-building.md) helps organize a property discussion; the [estimate example](../examples/guides/compare-septic-estimates.md) helps expose missing scope. Neither needs to become another general introduction to septic systems.

Write a specific, accurate title. A suggested meta description should summarize the page; it is not a promise that a search engine will display that text. Google explains that [title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link) and [snippets](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet) can be generated from multiple page signals. Do not promise a particular result appearance.

## Give links a reader purpose

Link to the next related decision where it comes up, with words that describe the destination. An estimate guide can send someone back to permitting questions if their quotes assume different approval responsibilities. A service page can offer the planning guide to someone who is not ready to request an estimate.

[Google's link guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable) supports descriptive, contextual link text and crawlable links. In a production website, confirm that the CMS generates real links and that the destinations work. Repository-relative Markdown links must be mapped to actual published URLs; the example filenames are not proposed live Out West Septic routes.

Use the business name for a relevant service inquiry. Do not attach an exact-match keyword link to every heading, require links as a condition of adaptation, or disguise a promotional page as an independent review.

## Add local detail only when it changes the answer

The [permit example](../examples/guides/septic-permit-questions.md) compares two official entry points without inventing county fees or approval times. That is useful local substance. Replacing a county name in an otherwise identical sales page does not provide it.

Before adding another location page, identify a distinct reader need and current local evidence: a different authority, documented process, or approved real project. Combine overlapping topics when one page answers them well. Never infer soil suitability from a county label or turn a business's approximate service radius into a guaranteed boundary.

## Review, publish deliberately, and learn

Use the [owner checklist](owner-review.md), verify links and sources, and test the destination CMS's headings, title, description, mobile reading, and navigation. Frontmatter alone does not publish metadata, and adding Markdown to GitHub does not configure a business website for indexing.

After publication, use the site's own analytics and search-performance data, when available, to see what people read and ask next. Update unclear passages and changed facts. Record meaningful review dates only when the material was actually checked. No word count, posting cadence, schema type, or number of brand mentions guarantees results.

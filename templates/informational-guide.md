# Informational-guide worksheet

Editorial template only. Keep unresolved facts and research questions here, outside finished article copy.

## Define the useful result

Write one sentence describing what the reader can do after reading. Choose a concrete output, such as a property-information file or a scope-comparison worksheet. Check existing pages for overlap.

## Build the draft

| Part | Writing prompt |
| --- | --- |
| Title and description | What specific question will this guide answer? |
| Opening | Why does this decision arise, and what useful action comes next? |
| Context | Which few verified facts does the reader need, with direct source links? |
| Method | What sequence of questions or actions helps without pretending to design, permit, or price the project? |
| Practical aid | Can the reader take a table, checklist, or written brief into a conversation? |
| Limits | Which decisions need an authority, qualified professional, or owner confirmation? |
| Next step | Which related page or honest business inquiry follows naturally? |

Label hypotheticals as exercises. Do not turn them into customer stories. Keep exact fees, technical dimensions, and exemptions out unless current authoritative evidence and appropriate context support them.

## Review the lesson

Write an annotation explaining what is original, which facts rely on sources, why the headings follow that order, and how another installer should adapt it.
